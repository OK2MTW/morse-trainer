          Sound and speed

          Character speed
          Spacing (Farnsworth)
          Tone
          Volume

          Spacing (Farnsworth) is shortened only after the full set: after 2 sessions ≥ 90 % with a stable reaction by one step, up to 20/20 WPM. With persistently low accuracy it gets longer. A manual change applies – the coach then waits for new data.

          Below 15 WPM characters stop sounding like a whole and tempt you to count dots. 20 WPM is recommended.

          Lesson length (block)

          5–10 min recommended. After 30 min of continuous practice the coach suggests stopping, above 60 min a day it warns.

          Telegraph key (experimental)

          Connect the HW key CwKey (USB) and press a paddle.

          Sending needs the HW key: it forms the dots and dashes itself, the app reads them and decodes. Without the key you can still listen and train receiving. A new key or a key that does not respond: see the CwKey firmware card below and the key manual.

          Key manual (GitHub)

          Sending lesson

          –

          The decoder learns your rhythm from the neighbouring elements (dot and dash lengths and the gaps inside characters, between characters and words) – you do not have to keep the set tempo. Fix a mistake with 7 or more dots – it erases the word in progress, or the word just finished.

          How the decoder reads

          The last words gap by gap, in dots of your key. Inside a character the key keeps 1 dot; between letters you should leave about 3, between words about 7. Gaps marked with ⚠ are close to a threshold.

            Recording for tuning the decoder: write what you sent, then send it.

            HW key CwKey (USB)

            A key with its own keyer and buzzer (a USB keyboard): the key forms the dots and dashes, the app reads only the stream key (default F13), decodes and evaluates. Speed and swapping the paddles are set on the key.
